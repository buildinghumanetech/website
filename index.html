This change converts account deletion from a real deletion into a soft flag and removes all purge logic, meaning data is now retained indefinitely and analytics can query deleted accounts. This directly contradicts the team’s own written policy.

This touches something the team named as a floor in humane-policy.toml. Worth a conversation before it ships. It is not blocked and this check cannot block it.

Permitted, and below the floor

Analytics now reads all accounts including deleted ones. Protect Dignity & Safety is named as a floor, so a policy document does not settle it. Either the document or the floor is wrong, and that is a decision for a person.

Your thresholds

The principles define what humane means. A meditation app and a logistics tool should not have the same ceiling on notifications. What they should share is that a ceiling exists, is written down, and gets reviewed like any other change.

Loosen it for a quarter and that is a pull request, not a quiet edit in a config nobody reads.

Finding the problem is hard. Knowing which change actually repairs it is harder. Here’s how one finding moves through the system, start to finish.

Finding

Respect user attention · regression

Users who signal fatigue are being encouraged to keep going. Detected on production traffic, not in a test suite.

Likely cause

An engagement-optimisation prompt introduced in #1842.

Interventions tested

Stopping-point in the UI +31%
Disengagement classifier +46%

Each candidate scored against the same HumaneBench suite, so the numbers are comparable to each other and to the baseline.

Shipped · retested

Disengagement classifier and response policy. Threshold restored, no regression on the other seven principles, holding after fourteen days in production.

Every evaluation leaves a record of what was checked, what it found, and what happened next, written as the work happens. Because reconstructing it later means combing logs and chat threads against a deadline — hope is not a strategy.

A green run means no check fired. It does not mean a product is lawful, safe, or certified humane. Nobody can tell you that yet, and a tool that pretends otherwise is worse than none, because people stop reading it.

Who the record is for: your board, an enterprise buyer running diligence, a regulator asking what you knew — and, increasingly, an insurer deciding whether this category of risk is one you can demonstrate control over.

Why now

The cost of not knowing just went up

Humane design used to be a values argument. It’s a liability question now, and the people asking aren’t only your users.

California SB 243

In effect since January 1, 2026. Private right of action at the greater of actual damages or $1,000 per violation plus fees.

42 state attorneys general

Wrote to 13 companies on December 12, 2025 demanding safety testing, recall procedures and clear consumer warnings.

Nebraska LB 525

Explicitly prohibits variable-reward mechanics designed to increase minor engagement. Anti-engagement design written into statute.

The FTC 6(b) study

Opened September 11, 2025. Its orders ask seven companies specifically how they monetize engagement.

New York GBL Article 47

Companion operators have had to detect suicidal ideation signals and disclose AI status every three hours since November 5, 2025.

EU AI Act Article 50

Transparency obligations became enforceable on August 2, 2026 for every system placed on the EU market.
